Is GPU radiation faster?

The atmospheric radiation code now runs on the A100 GPU and gives correct results. It is not yet faster. Here is the measurement, why, and what a real speedup would take.

Status as of 28 October 2026

Works

Correct on the GPU

The full radiation calculation runs on the A100 and matches the CPU result to machine precision. Validated end to end.

Not yet worth it

1.3× over the CPU

About 90 seconds per call on the GPU versus 120 on the CPU — and both are roughly 100× slower than the model's current radiation.

Why it is slow

The code walks through 256 narrow slices of the spectrum one at a time, in a loop on the ordinary processor.

Each pass does a small amount of arithmetic and a large amount of fixed setup: gathering inputs, handing a small job to the backend, collecting the result. That loop runs on the CPU in both versions. The GPU only speeds up the arithmetic inside each pass, which is the small part, so the GPU version spends most of its time idle, waiting on the loop that feeds it.

This also explains why the two backends are so close. They share the same ~90-second loop overhead on the CPU; the GPU just removes about 30 seconds of arithmetic on top of it. That ceiling is set by the structure of the code, not by the hardware.

What is not the problem

  • Data transfer. Moving arrays between the processor and the GPU costs about 0.2 of the 90 seconds. The bridge boundary is not the bottleneck.
  • GPU settings. Tuning block sizes and similar knobs would move the needle slightly, not close a 100× gap.

What a real speedup needs

Compute all 256 spectral slices together on the GPU in one pass, instead of 256 separate passes driven from the processor. That removes the per-slice setup and finally gives the GPU enough work to do at once to be worth using.

It is a substantial rewrite of the gas-optics and solver code, but the target is well defined and the correctness work already done carries straight over — the answers are known to be right, so the rewrite only has to reproduce them faster.

Where this leaves us

  • Done: the radiation port runs on the GPU and is numerically correct.
  • Open: whether to invest in the restructuring that turns "runs on the GPU" into "faster on the GPU."
